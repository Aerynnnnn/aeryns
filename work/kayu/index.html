02 How a piece is made

Seven weeks, four stages, one pair of hands.

This is the Lorong chair's timeline. Bigger pieces take longer; the order never changes.

  1. Week 1 · The timber

    We pick the boards before we pick up a tool.

    Our wood comes out of old buildings: joists, floorboards and door frames from shophouse restorations around the island, bought by the beam. It is pulled of nails, stacked with spacers and left to settle in the workshop for at least a year, so it moves in our shed and not in your house.

    You can come and choose the actual boards for a table top. Most people do.

  2. Weeks 2–4 · The joinery

    Cut by hand, held by wood.

    Legs and rails are joined with mortise and tenon, drawers with hand-cut dovetails, chair spindles with wedged through-tenons. Where a joint needs extra strength we drive in a chengal peg. Glue helps; nothing metal holds a Kayu Lane piece together.

  3. Weeks 5–6 · The finish

    Three coats of oil, rubbed in by hand.

    We sand to 240 grit, raise the grain with a damp cloth, then sand again. The finish is a plant-based hardwax oil, three coats a day apart, buffed by hand. It is food-safe, it doesn't peel like varnish, and a scratch can be oiled out at home.

    The maker's name and the build month are burnt into the underside.

  4. Week 7 · To your door

    Wrapped in old blankets, carried in by us.

    We deliver with our own van and two people, place the piece, and take every bit of packing away with us.

06 Care & lifetime repairs

If one of our joints ever loosens, we fix it free. For life.

That's a promise to the piece, not the receipt: it holds if you sell it, hand it down or find it at a flea market. Bring it in, or we collect on the island for RM60.

Every week

Dust with a dry cotton cloth. Wipe spills straight away; a damp cloth is fine, sprays and polish aren't.

Every 6–12 months

Rub in a thin coat of oil, wait 15 minutes, buff off the rest. Our Minyak Kayu oil is RM45 for 250 ml, about four tables' worth.

In Penang weather

Keep pieces 30 cm away from aircon vents and out of afternoon sun through west windows. Hairline gaps in the dry months are the wood breathing, and close again in the rains.
